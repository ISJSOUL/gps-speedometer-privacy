Speedometer: HUD, Odometer — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

This policy covers the Android app Speedometer: HUD, Odometer (package com.speedopro.app), previously published as GPS Speedometer. It is referred to below simply as “the app”.

What the app does with your data

Location (GPS speed). The app reads your device's GPS location — or, if you connect one, an external Bluetooth GPS receiver's (see below) — to calculate and display your current speed, altitude, and distance travelled.

  • Your location is never sent to us — we operate no servers and collect nothing about you.
  • Your raw location is not stored anywhere off your device.

Trip history (stored on your device). If you tap "Start" to record a trip, the app saves that trip's summary — duration, distance, minimum/maximum/average speed, minimum/maximum altitude, the weather at the time, a human-readable place name for where the trip started and ended (for example "Main St, Springfield"), and the route you travelled as a simplified list of coordinates so the trip can be drawn on a map. This is stored only in the app's private storage on your device, is never uploaded, and is deleted when you clear trip history or uninstall the app.

What happened during a trip (stored on your device). A recorded trip also keeps a short list of things the app noticed along the way, each with the coordinates and the time it happened: speed cameras, toll plazas and level crossings you passed and how fast you were going as you passed them; each time your speed went over the posted limit, together with that limit; and any map correction you chose to send to OpenStreetMap. This is what the "What happened" list on a saved trip is showing you. Like the rest of trip history it lives only in the app's private storage on your device, is never uploaded to us or to anyone else, and goes away when you delete the trip, clear trip history, or uninstall the app.

External GPS receiver (Bluetooth, optional). If you switch on "External GPS receiver" in Settings, the app connects to the Bluetooth GPS receiver you pick from the devices already paired with your phone, and reads the position, speed and satellite information it sends. On Android 12 and later this needs the "Nearby devices" permission, which the app asks for only when you switch the feature on. The app does not scan for other devices. What the receiver sends is used on your device exactly like the phone's own GPS, and is never sent to us or to anyone else.

Drive score, timers and the g-force meter (on your device). The drive score is worked out on your device from the trip it belongs to. The acceleration, sprint and braking timers save their results on your device. The g-force meter reads your phone's motion sensors only while it is on screen; its readings are not stored or sent anywhere.

Voice alerts (optional). If you turn on voice alerts, short fixed phrases such as "Speed limit 60" or "Toll ahead" are passed to your phone's own text-to-speech engine to be spoken. They contain no personal information.

Home screen widgets. If you add one of the app's home screen widgets, it shows the current trip's speed, distance and time, or your last trip's summary and your best acceleration time. That information is read from what is already stored on your device and is handed only to your device's launcher so it can draw the widget. Nothing extra is collected and nothing leaves your device.

Background location while recording a trip. While a trip is recording, the app keeps reading your location even if you switch away from it, so the trip isn't lost. Android requires a permanent notification to be shown for the entire time this is happening, and recording stops as soon as you press Stop.

Third-party lookups. To show weather, posted speed limits and road alerts, nearby fuel and charging stations, and the map behind a recorded route, the app sends your approximate coordinates to these services. None of them receives any account, device, or advertising identifier — only a latitude/longitude, or the map tile numbers derived from it:

  • Open-Meteo (weather) — https://open-meteo.com/en/terms
  • OpenStreetMap Overpass API (posted speed limits, road alerts, and nearby stations when Photon has none) — sent to one of the public Overpass servers, tried in turn: overpass-api.de (FOSSGIS), maps.mail.ru (VK) and overpass.private.coffee. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors. https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy
  • Photon by komoot (nearby fuel and charging stations) — used only when you open Fuel & Charging. https://www.komoot.com/privacy
  • OpenStreetMap tile servers (map images for trip history) — requested only for a trip you are actually looking at, and cached on your device so the same map isn't fetched again. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors. https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy

Opening a place or route in another app. Some buttons hand locations to another app or website, and only when you tap them:

  • "Open route in Maps" gives Google Maps a trip's start, end and up to nine points along its route.
  • "Show route on OpenStreetMap" opens GraphHopper Maps (an OpenStreetMap route planner) in your browser with a trip's start, end and up to nine points along its route — https://www.graphhopper.com/privacy/
  • The directions button on a nearby fuel or charging station, and the map button beside an entry in a trip's "What happened" list, give one latitude/longitude to your map app, usually Google Maps.

Nothing is sent in the background, and what you do in that other app or site is covered by its own privacy policy rather than this one.

Sharing a trip

A saved trip can be shared as an image, as a GPX file, or as a .spd trip file. Each is created on your device only when you tap share, and goes only to the app you then choose in Android's share menu (a chat, email, cloud drive and so on) — never to us. The GPX and .spd files contain the trip's route and details, so share them only with people you mean to see where you drove.

A .spd file holds the complete trip and is encrypted so that it opens only in this app; any change to the file makes it refuse to open. When you open a .spd file someone sent you, the trip is shown on your device only: it is not added to your own trip history and nothing is sent anywhere.

Place names for trip history are produced by Android's built-in geocoding service. The app passes only the coordinates to that operating-system API and never to any service of our own; depending on your device and Android version, Android may resolve the name on the device or using its own provider.

You can revoke location permission at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Speedometer → Permissions; the speedometer simply won't be able to show a live reading without it.

Advertising

The free tier shows ads through Unity LevelPlay, an ad mediation service operated by Unity Technologies, which fills them from Unity Ads and Meta Audience Network. These may collect device identifiers (such as the Android Advertising ID) and use them for ad personalisation, subject to your consent choices. Where required by law (EEA, UK, California), the app shows a consent form (via Google's User Messaging Platform) before any personalised ads are requested, your choice is passed to the ad networks before any ad is requested, and you can change that choice at any time via the same form.

See their own policies for what they collect:

  • Unity (LevelPlay and Unity Ads) — https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy
  • https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy
  • Meta (Audience Network) — https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy/

Some optional extras — certain speedometer dials, HUD styles and backdrops, the faster acceleration finish lines, and the longer sprint distances and other braking speeds — can also be unlocked for a limited time by choosing to watch a rewarded ad. Those come through the same networks described above, and are only ever shown when you tap to ask for one.

Earlier versions of this app used Google AdMob. It no longer does, and no data is sent to AdMob.

Purchasing Premium removes all ads permanently.

Reporting map corrections

The app lets you report a wrong or missing speed limit, and a missing fuel or charging station, to OpenStreetMap. You always start this yourself, and nothing is sent until you press Send on a screen that shows you the exact wording first.

When you do send one, the following is transmitted to OpenStreetMap and becomes a public, permanent map note that anyone can read:

  • the coordinates the report is about
  • the speed limit you entered, or the kind of station
  • the road or area name produced by your device's geocoder
  • your direction of travel, and the GPS accuracy of the fix
  • the app's name, version and build number

Reports are sent anonymously. They are not tied to any account, device identifier or advertising ID, and the app sends no name, email or contact detail with them. There is no free-text box: every note is assembled from the choices above, so a report cannot carry personal information. Because map notes are public and permanent, please do not use this feature for anything you would not want published.

OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy — https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy

In-app purchase

The one-time Premium unlock is billed and processed entirely by Google Play Billing. We never see or store your payment card details — Google handles the transaction and gives us only a purchase confirmation token, used solely to unlock premium features on your device/account.

Data we do not collect

The app does not require an account and does not collect your name, email, contacts, photos, or files. We ourselves operate no servers and receive no data about you.

Note that if you consent to personalised ads, the ad networks above may use your advertising ID for cross-app ad personalisation, as described under "Advertising" above — you can withdraw that consent at any time via the consent form, or reset/delete your advertising ID in Android Settings.

If you use the in-app "Send feedback" option, your email app opens with a pre-filled message that includes your app version, Android version, and device model, so we can reproduce the issue. Nothing is sent unless you press send in your own email app.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from children.
